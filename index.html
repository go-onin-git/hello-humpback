The challenge

A whale watch lasts an hour. A whale's life story spans decades.

Whale watching is a reason to visit Newfoundland and Labrador: 46% of Canadian travellers call it important when choosing where to go. The province leads, but Nova Scotia and Prince Edward Island are closing in.

And the experience itself was fleeting. You see a whale, snap a photo, and the moment ends in a camera roll. Meanwhile, marine scientists need exactly those photos. A humpback's tail is as unique as a fingerprint, but researchers can't be on every boat.

The well-being of humpbacks is vital to tourism. What if tourism could be vital to the well-being of humpbacks?

How the AI works

One photo. Seconds later, a whale with a name.

AI isn't a feature of Hello Humpback. It is the experience. Without it, none of this happens at all.

  1. 01

    Recognize

    Happywhale's machine-learning engine reads the fluke's unique markings and matches it against a catalogue of more than 120,000 individual whales. It used to take trained specialists. Now it takes seconds.

  2. 02

    Retrieve

    The whale's scientific record comes back with it: every logged sighting, where and when, migrations between breeding and feeding grounds, even calves.

  3. 03

    Retell

    Our custom storytelling model, Moby Bic, is built on GPT-4.1 and fine-tuned on stories by human writers. It turns that record into a warm, true story in the voice of a place famous for its storytellers.

  4. 04

    Return

    Travellers can send their photo to Happywhale's research database. Each new sighting adds a chapter to the whale's story and emails everyone who follows it.

From the record Inferred from the record Storyteller's voice

The salty air was thick with promise as Ecume made her first recorded appearance in July of 1992, gliding through the Quebec waters with all the serenity of a driftwood log riding a gentle tide.

Decades floated by with hardly a whisper from Ecume. Then, in March of 2019, she turned up among the coral and rolling swells of Silver Bank, Dominican Republic. The clear tropical light glimmered off her barnacled back, a world away from the rocky cliffs she'd once passed.

But it was a Newfoundland fog that truly raised eyebrows. In July of 2025, a streak of white broke the surface near St. Lunaire-Griquet, trailing ribbons of spray as she travelled north. After more than thirty years and thousands of watery miles, there she was, Ecume, spinning another chapter into her life's grand odyssey.

Responsible by design

Imaginative enough to make you care. Disciplined enough to be true.

  • Grounded in science, not invention

    Every story is built from Happywhale's verified sighting record. Moby Bic's instructions and story structure were iterated with guardrails against hallucination.

  • Disclosed on every story

    The real sighting data sits right beneath each story, with a plain-language note that the story was AI-generated from it.

  • Human craft at the core

    The model was fine-tuned on whale stories written by people, and calibrated for tone so warmth never tips into fantasy.

  • Researchers in the loop

    Identification comes from Happywhale, a global marine research collaboration. Names given by researchers are kept.

  • Consent, one choice at a time

    Sending a photo to science, following a whale, and getting tourism news are three separate opt-ins. Nothing is assumed.

  • Good for the whales, too

    The AI makes no demands on the animals. It learns from photos people already take, and the site teaches responsible whale watching.
